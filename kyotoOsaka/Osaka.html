Osaka and Himeji

Todays plan was to explore Osaka and see what it has to offer. Osaka is more modern than Kyoto, and is much closer to Tokyo in feel, with a much more vibrant night life. A thing that is available to tourists is something called an "Osaka Amazing Pass" and is a ticket to a majority of popular locations in Osaka aswell as free transport on some metro lines. You will get your moneys worth if you explore Osaka and i highly recomend it. The first place i went to was just round the corner to where i was staying, and that was the Tsutenkaku tower, which is the main observation tower of Osaka, with a good view of the city, being able to see all the way to the sea, they had also an exhibition about Glico which is the company that makes Pocky, and was founded in Osaka, and also are the company that made the running man ad in Dotonbori. Once i admired the view i took the metro into the city to see the Osaka skybridge. Which is famous for the floating escalator inbetween two skyscrapers. Honestly though, that is a bit overrated, and you can barely see anything from the escalator itself, howeer, once you get up, there is a very good viewing deck, where you can see all around the city, with it being right next to the river in Osaka, you could see even further than at the tower. Once back down, i decided its about time to hit the castle. Which was the last castle to fall in the civil war, and started the Edo period (the 200 years of peace ive mentioned previously) With te castle being very well preserved on the outside, but it had been completly redone on the inside to be a museum, so it felt a bit weird to be walkig round a very modern interior, with the outside being so old and elegant. Most of the exhibition was about that war, and how the different lords played in the fighting, with many armour and swords on desplay to see.
I did some more exploring of the grounds, investigating the grounds and looking at the defences and how they were built, and once i was out of the grounds i ended up in a food hall of sorts, where in the basement they had a display of minitures done by a popular japanese model company, with entry being included within the pass, i couldnt say no to looking around. After that i decided to walk again back down to Dotonbori, as i had access to a small sailing trip on the river, it was ok, with it only being 20mins, with just a different angle on things id already seen. Im sure it wouldve been alot more interesting and informative if i knew japanese though. By this point though it was beginning to get dark, so i headed for home, where after a short rest i went out for dinner, where i ended up at a gyoza/chinese restaurant, the food was really good.

The next day was a daytrip day, and i chose the further of the two trips, as i had gotten up earlier than previously. Which was Himeji. Another town thats got a castle, and thats about it. So i headed over, just as i was bying the tickets i noticed that there was an english tour starting in 3minutes, s i decided to join them on the tour. And im glad i did! It was nice to have someone that knew the place and could answer some questions i had. Himeji is regarded as the best preserved castle.with it still having the original interior and design. One impressive thing about the castle is that there are two central pillars going through the center of the 50m castle, one of them still being the original from 400years ago. It is a single log going all the way, and by some estimates was 600years old when it was cut down, so it now over a 1000years old, that tree has seen alot in its time. But the castle is indeed beautiful, with a stark white colour, and many keeps, it was impressive to see. The tour did take about two hours, and afterwards i headed to a neighbouring gardens as it was included in the tickets. It was pretty, with different sections for different kinds of plants and garden designs. With a large pond in the center with maybe 100 different carps. By this point though i was getting hungry, so i headed over to grab some lunch, and i neded up at a small restaurant run by a bunch of elderly people, and they were really kind, and served my some really amazing pork cutlets. By this point i decided its worth going back to my hostel and chilling for a bit, so thats what i did, had a shower and did some boring housekeeping. I did however go out and find some even better gyozas made by a sweet couple, and they really were delicious. Tommorow is Kobe

Kobe

Today i chose to go to Kobe, which is a port town, and is known around the world for its Wagyu, so i had only one thing on my to-do list, and that was to try some. But i had to fill in the time between getting there and dinner.
Kobe is located on the coast, and was an important port town connecting the western world with japan, so i decided it makes sense to start my exploration there. Once i was there, the first thing that i ended up reading about was a plaque commemorating the deadly earthquake that hit here. Slightly further down the key there was a memorial. This earthquake was the first earthquake to hit japan and be greater than a 7 on the richter scale in recorded history. So the area was not equiped to deal with the damage that it cause, the trauma can still be felt in places. With a piece of that key still being shown to show the damage that it causes. It very much hit just how unpredicatable the world is, and that it could happen at any moment. Luckily Japan has geared p to be able to withstand such earthquakes and everything has been engineered to withstand the shakes. I then proceeded down the pier and decided to go into the maritime museum, which turned out to be alot bigger than expected, with instalations about the development of ships, different kinds of ships, how ports work, and a collaberations with the Kawasaki company, where they displayed their history and invetions, it was crammed with students doing an excursion, but was still fun to see the motors and side projects theyve done in their time. Afterwards i had to go up the Kobe port tower, which could be seen all te way from the station, with 5 floors, this was the best tower ive gone up until now, with a good observation deck, a random lights instalation, and, as all good towers have, a rotating cafe. All the towers in the area were doing a collab with some new mobile game, so i ordered a funky bery float as it looked intersting, and i got a free coasted/merch thingy alongside it, woo (?). I ended up being there for longer than expected, so i didnt have much time to do much else, except for walk down the last bit of the port area and turn round and begin heading back into the center of town to find somewhere to eat. As i was walking back i stopped by a butchers that also did food, and decided they probably knew what they were doing, and they did indeed. I did end up paying shy over 10k yen for some meat, but it was so good. Probably the best steak ive ever had (sorry parents, yours are also good xxx). With two different cuts, i could taste the difference. I never really understood people saying it "melts in your mouth" when it came to wagyu, but i get it now, it really does. But sadly all good steaks come to an end, and i had to eventually pay, even if i did procrastinate for a while. The train ride home was quiet, but my day today ws surprisingly packed, with both the museum and tower taking me by surprise by how interesting and good they were, and i came home a happy man, and with it being my last day here in Osaka, i had managed to do everything i had set out to do.
