Tsuyama to Fukuoka

Tsuyama and Okayama

Today the plan was to move to Tsuyama, which is a small town in central Japan, due to the mountains in the area, there wasnt any trains taking me directly there, so instead i had to get a coach. I had booked a ticket to tsuyama the day before, for what i thought was noon, but once i got there, it turns out it was arriving at tsuyama at noon 😅. So i talked to the ticket desk, and they allowed me anyways onto the bus, which i was very grateful for. The timetable did say it would take about 3hours, but due to heavy traffic in Osaka it was closer to 4 and a half. so i arried in Tsuyama at 1630, which is unfortunate since all the toursity places shut at 17:00, and since it takes about 20mins to walk to the hotel there was no chance of doing that today, so the plans were pushed to tomorrow. Instead i checked into the hotel, turns out i was the only person here, the hotel itself is really modern and clean, which was nice. Instead of seeing the castle i took a walk into the centre, and wandered around, i took a detour to a park that was nearby, since that was kinda the only point of interest that was still open. But by that point it was getting late, so i headed back towards the center from the gardens in search of food. I ended up at a small restaurant, that was very unasuming on the outside. But the food was so good. I started with gyozas, but instead of the normal dough wrapping it was chicken skin, which was an interesting change to the normal, aswell as crispy chicken. Due to the translation of the menu, i overestimated the size, so i asked the waiter for what they recomend, and they talked about a Hot pot style meal. So thats what i had, It is very similair to a hot pot, except its already been cooked, so you have all the ingreadients allready ready to eat. That was really nice and fresh, and i had no complaints there. After i walked home, and decided that since this hotel had a bath, i might aswell try it out. Being Japan though it took me about 10minutes to figure out what the 15 different buttons, knobs, switches and screens do. But eventually i figured it out and relaxed, ready for the next day.

The next day i woke up bright and early to do some exploring of the town again. I first checked out and lugged my suitase back ot the station to leave it at a coin locker. This is something i feel is under appreciated in Japan, and that is that every station has coin lockers, which makes it so much easier if you have a stop over, and its cheap! So i left that and walked over to the castle, along the way i stopped by a small hole in the wall place that was selling red and white bean filled pancakes, for only 100 yean each, it was run by an elderly couple and i had to try one of each, this bean paste is very common in japan, and even if it is a little savoury, its soething that you will find on anything to make it a dessert.As i munched on that i walked over to the castle, which is actually more of a ruin where they rebuilt one of the turrets to house some info about the castle. It had all the usual history and how it was important in the civil war in the 17th century, but the views of the town were the best around. Not far from the castle Tsuyama also had a Museum, which went over the history of Tsuyama and what happened in the area, aswell as many statues and replicas of ancient artifacts, aswell as a dinosaur :) But overall it was quite average. Afterwards i decided its worth going to find some lunch, and nearby there was a small cafe, run by some pensioners, they were really sweet and tried to help me the best they could with their lack of english, but i ordered a crouquette meal, and it was massive, and i really struggled to finish it, but it was really good, and i couldnt dissapoint them, so i powered through, along with a coffee, it cost me only 1200yen.
It was getting to around 2pm at this point, so i decided its worth heading back, and if i did i would comfortably catch the next train to Okayama. The train arrived and it was really old train, with what felt like table fans strapped to the ceiling as AC, but the seats were soft and the nature pretty, so i didnt really complain, and the journy was over before i knew it. Okayama is a much more modern and youthful city, and with me staying at a hostel just 10mins away from the central station, i was surronded by hip stores and thrifting places, so i spent a while looking through them as i walked over the Okayama castle, which was about 3km away. Originall the plan was t do the castle and the gardens on the same day, but i only had time for 1, so i did the castle. This castle is unique for its black exterior and unique panelling, which made it look very mysterious. But it had been completely rebuilt in the 60s, so it was again made as a museum, but the exhibit was actually quite interesting, there was a very famour battle that happneded here between 3 generals, where one backstabbed the eastern army during the civil war, that turned the tide in the war. But they went into detail about each general, their motives and past life, to get a really good feel of how it woldve been like to be one of them, it was very imersive and interesting way of doing it.
Once the castle closed i began the alk home and decided to have dinner in the hostel as they had some intersting things on the menu. So i went at sat at the bar (as it was the only place available) and i ended up chatting with a local and a french guy thats in okayam for en exchange, it was very interesting to talk to them and i had a nice night with them. But eventually i deicided its probably best to get a good nights sleep before travelling to fukuoka. So thats what i did

Fukuoka Day 1 and 2

Today the plan was to explore Okayama and go to Fukuoka, so i got up, and left the hostel relatively early, and walked over to the station to leave my luggage there for the morning, while i was there i grabbed some breakfast. I had seen that there was a bakery called "the little mermaid". Which was obviously taking insporation from a danish bakery, so i had to try it out in the name of science. I did have to instantly deduct a couple points for not having any cinamon products whatsoever, but apart from that the cakes and sandwhiches were relatively nice and good for the price. I then walked the 20minutes over to to the castle and then gardens. The gardens were originally built in the 17th century to replicate rolling fields, with succesive lords of the castle adding onto it with what was fashionable at the time. This leading to a patchwork of different gardens. With still some of the old fields existing in the middle of the garden. It was very pretty and worth the detour to go there. Across from the garden there was the Okayama prefacture museum, which had alot of similair artifacts to the tsuyama one, except more, older and more important. There was an exhibition of statues and paintings of the 36 immortal poets, being some of the famous poets of the ancient japanese world. And it was the first time that all statues had been collected in one place, So that was interesting to look and research about. By this point it was getting towards 2pm, so i decided to head back. When i arrived at the station, something that i would never thought of happened. The shinkansens were delayed, and some of them cancelled. The entire station was in chaos, as every train between Osaka and Fukuoka was halted for over 2 hours, I had arrived towards the end of the of the stoppage, but still when i got to the ticket booth, there was 90 people standing in queue infront of me. So i hunkered down in a corner and waited an hour to get my ticket.
I had decided for the next week to get an area pass, since ill be using the shinkansen and ferries a decent amount, so using that i reserved a seat on a bullet train once my turn was up. Unfortunately, i misunderstood the ticket and the person at the desk, as the train departed at 1445, not that the train was originally meant to leave at 14:45, this meant as i was taking my sweet time sitting down and having a nice lunch, the train left. I only realised afterwards, which is annoying. I decided this time to use the machines and figured out eventually how to use them. Unfortunatenly that meant i had to wait about 90mins for my train, since the machine gave me a ticket for a train that would depart after the current time. So i stood around in the cold for another 90minutes before finally getting on my train. While on the train, i got a message from a reoccuring character now. The french guy from Tokyo and Osaka, he was in Fukuoka, so we decided to meet up for dinner, he had found a nice tsukemen ramen place, and it was indeed really nice, with a rich broth and the noodles were cooked to perfection. This was his last night in japan before flying to Seoul. So it was good that it was a good location. It turned out that he also was staying at the same hostel as me, so we began heading back. However we decided that it would be a waste not to do something before he left. So we went over to round 1. The arcade place that here had a bunch of other sports that you could do, we decided on table tennis. as we both were saying how good we were. So we booked an hour of that and played for a good 50mins of it before both tapping out. But it was good fun! We then headed down to the racing sims, and played mariokart for a few rounds because he suggested it and said it was fun, and indeed it was. He did eventually win 3-2. But i totally let him win. After we decided to finally head home as it was getting close to midnight. I said goodbye to him in the lift then promptly went to bed.

I gave myself a bit of a lie in, as it was a late night before. With the plan today to walk around Fukuoka. I started with heading just over the road to the castle ruins, where in the park next to it there was being held a dog festival/exhibition thing, so there were so many dogs running around enjoying the sun. I grabbed myself a coffee and continued towards the castle. It itself was ok, with good views of the city, but nothing exceptional. I then headed over to shrine on a hill overlooking the harbour. Since it was only half an hour walk away, i did that. I walked up, down and around the hill ending on the harbour side. So i then chose to walk along the harbour front towards what i thought was a observation tower in the distance, and after some research it was indeed. It was still early enough in the day, so i took the hour it would take to enjoy the breeze and maybe even have a cheeky ice-cream. It is an industrial port so alot of the water front was barriered off for obvious reasons. But i still enjoyed the bits that i could go along. Not long after i got to the tower and it was free to go up! So even better, the view from the tower towards the sea was very pretty, and with the sun glistening on the waves it was something. Once back down i headed towards the center to do a bit of shopping, as i had a few hours left before probably the thing i have been most looking forward to the whole trip.
Originally i wasnt planning on going to Fukuoka at all, however i saw that a band of mine that i really like FAKE TYPE. a modern electro-swing/electropop style band, with a relatively small fan base, was playing here this would be probably the only time ill be able to see them, as i doubt theyll ever do a tour out of japan, and this was the only day within my trip that they are playing. The only issue was, was that i couldnt purchase tickets online, as i didnt have a japanese phone number. So i wasnt even garenteed to get in. So i arrived early and asked the staff working there. They told me to wait, and they looked to see if noone showed up and luckily someone didnt! So i payed a couple thousand more than a regular ticket, was at the back of the venue, but it was worth it! Chills listening to them. The only complaint was that they do alot of talking and not as much playing, with them having a segment were they left the stage to do a "zoom call" where they tried to guess there own music from a short sample. This segment was close to 15-20mins and i felt like it was a bit unnecessary to have 10 songs they had to guess. But it could just be the culture with artists. Being a lot more friendly and conversationey, then just there to play music. Which i feel wold be nice, except i dont speak japanese well enough to understand what they were saying. Another fun quirk ive seen is that instead of people just dancing to the music as whats common in europe. They are told by the band what to do, how to wave your hands how to bop your hand, how fast etc. To every song, which leaves very little place to actually just jam to the music, and your arm gets very sore by the end, as its rude not to do it, but with your hands being up for over an hour it does get to you. But it definitely gives of a cool effect of the whole room dancing together, quite mesmerising. I did of course get a t-shirt after.
Since the concert started at 18:00 there was still time afterwards to grab dinner. I had found a nice ramen place tucked away inside a building, and i think it was in my top 3 favourite ramens of the trip. The egg especially was the best ive had, with it being marinated in a really rich umami flavour before hand. Once i had finished that i headed back to the hostel, when i got there it turns out there was a "cultural exchange event" where there were cheap drinks and you were encouraged to talk to a bunch of people, so i happily obliged and spent a couple hours mingeling with all sorts of people, and it was nice to listen to different peoples experiences and how theyve ended up here. After that though it was properly late and i headed to bed in preperation for the next day.

Fukuoka Day 3 & 4

Today i chose to go out on a day trip and continue to use my travel pass, so i went north to the northern most town on the island, Kitakyushu. with it only being 15minutes by bullet train (but traveling 80km) it made sense to go there. Arriving there first place to go to was obviously the castle. It was built, like most castles. At the start of the 17th century to defend in the civil war. It honestly very interactive with alot of different things to do, mostly for the kids but still fun nonetheless. It is well known for two giant floor to ceiling tigers that are on the inside walls of the castle, with them being drawn in sush a way that they look like they are looking at you from all angles. It did have a nice view of the city, but generally just another castle in another city. However the grounds did have a very pretty garden, right in the middle of the city, which gave a tranquil feeling inside of a generally quite busy city.Inside of the gardens there was an exhibition of traditional industries. More specifically on kimono weaving and Kintsugi, the japanese art of fixing broken pottery with gold. It was really in depth and interesting to read about and see how it is made. The ticket did include entrance to the japanese litracy museum, but i wasnt particulary bothered by it at the time. I then wandered back into town, as there was a big anime and other japanese culture mall that i wanted to visit. More specifically it had a manga and anime museum (yes i see the hypocrisy in it now, shhh). As kitakyushu has a rich history in manga, boasting one of the densest concentration mangakas in japan. With the most famous being Leiji Matsumoto. Aswell as an entire library dedicated to mangas where you could sit and read what felt like every manga ever made. You can see in one of the images that even the escalator handrail has got sketches from the anime that he made. I did also enjoy the plethora of nerdy shops for me to browse. I decided to begin heading back as i had messaged a few people via Hostel world and we have decided to meet up for dinner. There was two of us in the end, a dutch guy and a swiss girl, we originally struggled to find a place as a couple of the places we went to were either full or closed. Evenetually we found a place, and it was... interesting. It had decent reviews, but it sold a different kind of ramen, with what i think was a tomato base, and served with cheese. To be frank i didnt particularly like it, it had bits of meat, that were quite unapealling, and the flavour was weird. But i ate it anyways. We then walked back together, as we all were staying in the same hostel. We grabbed some drinks along the way and chilled in the common room and gossiped until 1am. We then decided to join up together tomorrow for some exploring.

Tomorrow rolls around, and i was sure we decided to meet up at 9:30 after breakfast to leave, so i diligently got up even if i didnt have so much sleep, and waited. An hour later i got a message from the dutch guy that hes just woken up and heading for a shower and then he'll come down. So not the early start i had envisioned. It did give me the chance to write more of my blog and do some laundry, so that was a good thing done at least. By 7pm i gave up and headed out to at least find some dinner, I found a Yakatori, a japanese restaraunt that does skewers. And i was hungry so i loaded up on that and salad. I then headed home and slept as there wasnt much more to do today. Tomorrow is a long day ahead of me to Okayama.

Matsuyama day 1 & 2

Today the plan was to travel up to Matsuyama, a large town on the large island of Shikoku south of Hiroshima, I chose to go there as the area pass i have allows me to take the ferry there for free, and normally it costs 6000 yen each way, so its a good use of the pass. So i start of by taking the bullet train across to Hiroshima, with no problems this time :) Seems like it was only a one day issue. In Hiroshima i couldnt decide to between taking the train to Kure or going straight to Hiroshima. eventually i decided to go from Hiroshima as thats probably easiest. So i took the bus from the station down to the harbour, where i just missed the ferry over, so i had an hour to kill until the next one.
The ferry was a fast ferry only taking 90minutes across the water. The boat was comfy and with the sun blazing down, i had to put on my sunglasses inside as to not be blinded. Something that i find really unique to the japanese geography is just how bumpy the geography is. With tons of small islands everywhere you look, just large enough for a couple trees, but that does mean that it makes it quite hard for boats to navigate the waters. But it does mean that as a passenger the views are breathtaking. Eventually though the time comes for us to arrive at the port, and from there it was a quick walk to a train into the actual city, and from there a bus to the hotel. This is when i realised that i had booked a hotel in the busiest and most touristy area on the entire island. Matsuyama is famous for its hot springs, and more specifically dogo Onsen, where there was a huge queue outside to get in, and people wandering the area in their onsen clothes (i would say a cross between a kimono and bathrobe), i was luggin my suitcase along the cobbles inbetween them. But the hotel itself was fancy for my standards, with a lift up to my room, which is a luxury! The room unfortunately was a smoking room, as that was the only option they had. So the room had a bit of an aroma of old smoke, but it wasnt too strong and quickly i couldnt smell it anymore. But i didnt stay very long as it was already getting late. The travels took most of the day. I headed out for dinner, i found a place about 10minutes away, with extremely good reviews. And for good reason, the food was a amazing, cheap and the staff were sweet as always. Its the kind of place that you go in and see plastic chairs and plastic coverings and instead of feeling its cheap, you know they save everything for the food. I had okonomyaki, the japanese pancakes, and takoyaki, japanese savoury dough balls (æbleskiver for my danish readers), filled with octopus bits, its a very common street food, and is something ive been wanting to try for a while, and im glad i did! With a good beer to wash it down, i left a happy guy. I then wandered the area for a bit, but it is very touristy so i didnt stay for long, and decided to save the touristing myself to the next day

I woke up earlyish as i had to check out. I took my suitcase then over to the central station as it was only a 20 minute walk to the Matsuyama castle, this castle was on a big hill that i had to climb up. On the way the city had converted one of the courtyards on the slopes of the hill into a garden (this happened in the late 1800's) so i enjoyed the wander before continuing the climb up the hill. On the top there was another castle, with plenty of swords, samurai armour and a good view of the city. A fun fact i learnt is that matsu in japanese in Pine, and yama is mountain/hill, the original lord that built the castle on the hill planted pines on the hill, and they spread all over it. Hence the name. One thing i noticed as i explored the city is that they love oranges, you couldnt go 100meters without seeing a shop selling orange related food,drink or merch. With it being relatively southern and warmer climates, it boasts growing over 30 varieties of oranges. I did eventually cave and had a orange flavoured ice cream (it did come with a scoop or marmalade on the side, and that was delish). But the weather was a bit iffy, and i knew there was still some decent travel time to my hostel. So i wandered over to the station, got the train then walked over to the port. I was lucky to board a ferry just 10minutes after getting there. This time however it was the slow ferry, and this one took close to 4hours to get to Hiroshima, so just aswell i went early. From there i had to catch 2 buses to my hostel. But at least the buses were relatively frequent, so i didnt need to wait long to get there. The hostel is onlt 15mins away from the main station and seems to be run by 2 besties. The vibes here were really good. With the hostesses gossiping woth the people slepeing here, and generally it felt very homely (i mean it does look like its a converted house). So i decided i might aswell have dinner here, and chat with them, so i had their curry and it was really good! The i headed off to bed, as i plan on exploring Hiroshima, and theres alot to do there
